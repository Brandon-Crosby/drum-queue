How to tune kicks and 808s to your song's key

Low drums have a pitch, whether you meant them to or not. A kick in the wrong key makes the bass sound off; an untuned 808 can make the whole record feel out of tune. Tuning them takes two minutes.

Find the pitch

Load the sample and look at the sustained part, not the first few milliseconds. The attack of a kick or 808 usually starts higher and drops quickly; the note you hear is where it settles.

  • With a tuner plugin: put a tuner on the channel and play a long note. Most DAWs include one.
  • With a spectrum analyzer: the tallest low peak is the fundamental. Compare it to the table below.
  • By ear: play the sample against a sine or bass note on the keyboard and move the note until they stop beating against each other.

One octave up doubles the frequency: C2 is 65.4 Hz, A2 is 110 Hz. (Octave numbers here follow the common scientific convention where middle C is C4; your DAW may label them differently, but the frequencies are the same.)

Tune it

  • Kick: tune the body to the song's root note or its fifth. Use the sampler's coarse pitch in semitones, then fine-tune in cents. Most kicks only need a move of a semitone or two; big shifts change their character.
  • 808: set the sampler's root note to the pitch you measured. Then playing a key plays that note: write the 808 line on the piano roll like a bass part.

Pitch-shifting in a sampler also changes length: tuning down makes a sample longer and slower, tuning up makes it shorter. For 808s that's usually fine. For kicks, if a tuned kick feels too long, shorten its release.

Kick and 808 together

When they hit at the same time, they should agree. Common setups: kick tuned to the root, 808 playing the root on the same beat; or kick tuned a fifth above. If the low end sounds "wobbly" when they overlap, they're slightly out of tune with each other. More on the kick and 808 relationship.

Sampled kicks from songs

A kick taken from a song is tuned to that song's key. Before you use one from a kit you've made, measure it and retune it to yours, then put the note in the file name ("Kick - F.wav") so you never have to measure it again.
